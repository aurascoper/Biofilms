# ART questions for Ellen

The paper supports a public-data presence study and a short list of biochemical questions. It does not yet support putting ART into the survival or phase equations.

## Questions to take to the meeting

1. Is MIC the right applied endpoint: persistence and turnover of surface biofilm lineages, with phage predation as a possible driver?
2. Which public wet-storage samples are close enough to MURR chemistry and materials to be useful comparators? Which must remain separate strata?
3. For a later bench study, which beneficiary matters: the bacterial host, an infected cell that arrests or dies, the infecting phage, or a phage competing with it?
4. Which first assay would distinguish function: ART-dependent cDNA after infection, loss of a phage phenotype after catalytic-motif mutation, RT--partner binding, or partner-dependent toxicity?
5. Does the conserved RT `YxDD` motif support an active-site mutant as the cleanest causal control?
6. Does the long, variable N-terminal region bind the partner? Would domain deletion or a non-cognate partner swap test the predicted interface?
7. Do the 15--49 nt repeats and their approximately 15 nt palindromic cores define processing boundaries or RNA structures? Which repeat or stem mutations preserve transcription while disrupting the proposed structure?
8. Are the 120--220 nt variable spacers templates, baits or recognition elements? Does spacer order matter?
9. Type I partners have two GNAT-like folds, while types II and III are unrelated helical proteins. Is there one shared output or several systems grouped by the RT lineage?
10. What evidence would justify moving from presence to mechanism: RT activity, an RNA substrate, partner interaction and a named phenotype with a named beneficiary?

## Boundary for the preprint

Call ART a newly described phage-encoded architecture and a candidate future question. State that the reported hosts do not overlap the seven-species panel, and that the paper reports no radiation, stress, biofilm, growth or survival phenotype. Keep DRT2 as an external precedent showing that individual-cell loss can protect a population. Do not describe ART as radioresistance, phase locking or host defence.
