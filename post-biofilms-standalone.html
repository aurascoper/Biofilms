Eleven of Sixteen Runs Agreed With a Number I Typed In Myself

Eleven of sixteen. That is how many random seeds reproduce the ordering in a published biofilm figure, and on the eleventh of September I rendered the simulation a hundred times to find that out, one frame every four Monte Carlo steps out to four hundred, because I only meant to watch seven species sort themselves in three dimensions. What I got instead was a lesson about what one run of a stochastic model is worth. I got it the hard way, which is by being wrong twice before lunch.

Here is the animation. Seven species, twenty voxels on a side, eight thousand lattice sites, four hundred steps, seed 42.

The repository this comes from is unusually careful about where numbers come from. Every parameter carries a provenance row naming its source paper or declaring that it has none. Every committed figure carries a checksum sidecar and a text extraction, so a claim cannot hide inside an image. Every withdrawn number has a ledger entry recording what was wrong and how it was caught. It is a chain of custody for data, maintained better than most published work manages.

What I ended up testing was whether there is a chain of custody for confidence, and there is a link missing near the end of it.

The cast, and one letter

The model is a three-dimensional cellular Potts simulation of a mixed biofilm under a radiation field. Seven organisms: Cryptococcus neoformans, Deinococcus radiodurans, Cladosporium sphaerospermum, Bacillus subtilis, Aspergillus niger, Shewanella oneidensis, Ochrobactrum intermedium.

Three of them make melanin. Two of them sit in a set the code calls RADIOTROPIC.

That set used to be called RADIOTROPHIC. The rename is one letter and it is the entire point. Radiotropism is directional growth toward a source, a real measured phenomenon. Zhdanova put soil micromycetes in front of a cadmium-109 source in 2004 and measured mean return angles under ninety degrees. Radiotrophy is the much larger claim that ionizing radiation enhances growth, that something out there makes a living off gamma. This model implements the first. It cannot implement the second, for a reason that took me an afternoon to appreciate properly.

The field starts, as everything in this field starts, on the walls of the damaged reactor at Chernobyl, where somebody noticed that the fungi growing on the concrete were unusually black. One wall, one observation, and a literature that has been arguing about the generalization ever since. Keep that shape in mind, because I am about to do a smaller version of it and get caught.

Nothing grows

I measured occupancy across the run before I measured anything else, and I am glad I did, because it reframed the whole animation.

At step 4 the biofilm occupies 1,612 of 8,000 sites. At step 400 it occupies 1,633. That is 1.3 percent over four hundred steps, and it is the volume constraint breathing rather than anything living. Every individual species holds between 226 and 237 sites for the whole run, a band about five percent wide. Nothing divides and nothing dies, and that is a property of the code rather than a reading of those numbers: divide_cell! has no trigger and no cell is ever removed. The constant totals show net exchange near zero. They cannot show that territory goes back to whoever lost it, which would need labelled transition histories I did not compute.

What the animation shows is rearrangement at fixed biomass, seven fixed budgets of material, shuffling.

Which is why radiotrophy cannot be in this model, and why one letter is load-bearing rather than pedantic. Radiotrophy is a statement about growth rate. There is no growth here for it to be a statement about. The parameter comment in the source says so without decoration: "Not damage, not benefit: the model has no death and no growth to express either in."

The claim I went to check

The three melanin producers each secrete at a hand-set rate called α_M. C. sphaerospermum 0.14, C. neoformans 0.10, A. niger 0.065. Melanin diffuses, it accumulates, and it feeds back into which moves the simulation accepts, so a species sitting in melanin-rich sites is partly sitting in something its neighbors secreted.

The repository publishes a figure of exactly this, mean melanin at each producer's occupied sites out to step 100, and the figure carries a disclaimer printed inside the image, in grey, under the axis.

Ordering follows the hand-specified alpha_M_species scales; not a measured quantity

This is good practice and rarer than it should be. Somebody wrote the caution into the artifact itself, where a screenshot cannot separate it from the plot. The README says the same thing in prose. The ordering among the three producers is set by the input.

Fine. So my question was narrower and, I thought, boring. How reliably does one run actually display it?

Being wrong, first pass

Seed 42, four hundred steps, mean melanin at each producer's sites.

C. sphaerospermum 4.58. A. niger 3.48. C. neoformans 2.37.

That is the wrong order. A. niger secretes at 0.065 against C. neoformans at 0.10, so it makes 35 percent less melanin per parcel and ended up sitting in 47 percent more of it.

I had a mechanism inside ten minutes and it was a good one. Melanin diffuses slowly and pools inward. I measured the correlation between melanin and distance from center across the three producers at −0.43, and A. niger had finished deeper in the aggregate than C. neoformans, mean radius 5.19 against 6.71. Position beats production. The input ordering is a well-mixed intuition and this is not a well-mixed system.

Tidy. I was pleased with it. Three more seeds killed it. On seed 43 the inversion still happened, but C. neoformans was the inner species that time and still carried less melanin, so the mechanism I had reached for did not survive its first real test.

Sixteen seeds

At four seeds I had a replacement story ready, that C. neoformans was simply the unstable one, ranging wider than the others. That is an artifact of four seeds. So much for that theory too, and I went to sixteen.

Sixteen seeds, identical parameters, step 100, the same timepoint the published figure uses.

species α_M mean sd range
C. sphaerospermum 0.140 2.192 0.274 1.603 – 2.587
C. neoformans 0.100 1.647 0.296 1.098 – 2.139
A. niger 0.065 1.274 0.162 0.943 – 1.540

The means come out in the input's order. The model recovers what was put into it. The disclaimer is correct.

And eleven of the sixteen individual runs reproduce that ordering. Four put A. niger above C. neoformans. One put C. neoformans above C. sphaerospermum. The gap between the two lower producers is 0.373. Divide by a pooled standard deviation of 0.238 and the separation comes to 1.57, but the pooled figure is the wrong denominator, and it flatters the result. The three species share a lattice in every run, so these are sixteen paired comparisons, not thirty-two independent draws. Paired, the spread of the per-seed difference is 0.412, where independence would have predicted 0.337. Those two species are negatively correlated within a run, r = -0.59 across the sixteen seeds, so pooling understated the uncertainty rather than overstating it.

Split by pair, the count says something a single number hides. C. sphaerospermum above C. neoformans holds in 15 of 16 seeds, an exact two-sided sign test at p = 0.00052. C. neoformans above A. niger holds in 12 of 16, p = 0.077, which does not reject a coin flip at the conventional level. The ordering that fails is the one between the two closest producers, and one sample cannot reliably tell you which of those two is which.

Five runs in sixteen display a different ordering from the one the disclaimer attributes to the input. That is not a contradiction of the parameter file. The file sets production coefficients; the figure plots a mean over currently occupied sites, and diffusion and spatially varying production sit between them. What the five runs contradict is the idea that one trajectory reliably displays the ordering.

Disclaimer inheritance

The published figure separates its two lower producers by 0.03, which is 0.97 against 0.94, two lines close enough to share a shaded band for the whole run. One seed, no error bar. (Its configuration is larger than mine, forty voxels a side and six parcels per species against my twenty and two, so the values are not directly comparable. The shape of the problem is.)

The disclaimer attributes the ordering to the input rather than to a measurement. True, and useful. But it invites a second assumption it never actually makes, and that nobody wrote down, which is that a figure honestly disclaiming itself as a display of an input is at least a faithful display of that input. Call it disclaimer inheritance, the reader inheriting confidence in the display from the author's candor about the source.

That is the missing link in the chain of custody. Provenance travels beautifully in this repository, from paper to ledger row to parameter to figure, and every step of it is checksummed. Then it reaches the last step, from parameter to this particular rendering of the parameter, and there is no custody at all, because one trajectory of a stochastic model is one sample and a disclaimer about provenance says nothing whatever about variance. The ordering was the input, and the input still lost, five times in sixteen.

Disclaimer inheritance is the more useful thing I found, because it is not specific to melanin or to this model. Any honestly-labeled single-run figure invites it. The label that would close it is an error bar, or a sentence giving n, rather than a better disclaimer about sourcing.

Two more things I could not unsee

The coupling constant that makes the melanin-to-movement mechanism work is k = 0.5. Its provenance row calls it dimensionless, evidence basis declared, and then says where it lives: "Hard-coded at the call site; absent from Table 2 and from every configuration file." Every quantity in this model that came from a paper has a row and a source. This one has a row saying it has no source. It is the parameter the mechanism is most sensitive to, and the one place in the repository where custody simply stops.

And β_ion, the radiation coefficient that makes RADIOTROPIC mean anything, is the smaller half of its own mechanism at the values the model ships. I did not discover that. The repository's own test did, and it had to cheat to see it. tests/deterministic_radiation.jl must deliberately amplify β to plus and minus 20 against a shipped 5e-5 before multi-seed drift becomes detectable at all. Melanin exceeds it by a factor of about ten in the acceptance functional. So the two species in the radiotropic set are, in practice, moving because of melanin, and the radiation term is along for the ride.

A factor of ten, and not more than that. I had "inert" in that paragraph until I checked. That would have been the same overstatement the repository already caught in itself. The code comment now reads "larger by a factor of 9.6 in ΔH, NOT by four orders" and carries the date it was corrected. Ten times smaller is small. It is not nothing, and I was one sentence from writing a post about provenance that rounded one to the other.

What this is not

Nothing above is evidence about real fungi, real melanin or real radiation. The coupling constant alone would prevent it from being that.

Nor is it a criticism of the disclaimer. That figure was already doing better than most, which is exactly why it is the useful example, and disclaimer inheritance needs an honest figure to demonstrate it on.

The model is not wrong either. The means recover the input. It does what it says.

And the result covers sixteen seeds at a single configuration, twenty voxels a side with two parcels per species, which is the exporter's default and smaller than the preprint's forty and six. Cellular Potts models in general are outside what I measured.

What would change my mind

  1. Sixteen seeds at the preprint's own configuration, forty voxels with six parcels. Larger parcels average over more sites and the variance should fall. If eleven of sixteen becomes sixteen of sixteen there, then this is a small-lattice artifact and the published figure is fine as drawn.
  2. A seed sweep at step 1000 rather than 400. The melanin field never saturates in this model, mean 0.011 rising to 0.916 across my run with no decay or substrate term, so separations may simply grow until the orderings stop crossing.
  3. Any argument that mean melanin at occupied sites is the wrong observable. I took it because the published figure plots it. The occupied-site mean is already volume-weighted, since every voxel counts once and a larger parcel therefore contributes more. The distinct observable is the mean of per-parcel means, weighting each parcel equally, and I have not tested one.

I would rather find these myself than have someone find them for me, which is most of why the sweep went to sixteen instead of stopping at the seed that told the better story.
